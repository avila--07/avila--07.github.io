Every new person adds more than one conversation

Watch a team grow one person at a time. In a flat team everyone stays in sync with everyone. Add layers, with squads, leads and a head, and the number of lines drops sharply, but messages now take more hops to get across.

In a flat team, coordination cost grows as n(n−1)/2. That's the core of Brooks's law: adding people to a late project makes it later, because each newcomer adds lines to everyone already there.

Layers cap that growth. Members only stay in sync inside their squad of up to five, leads sync with each other, and the head talks to the leads. The price is distance: a message between two members of different squads has to go up through a lead and back down, which adds latency and room for things to get lost along the way.
